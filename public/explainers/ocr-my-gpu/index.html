The big idea & current stage

Fitting a giant reader onto a tiny 8 GB desk

Vision-language models (like Qwen3-VL-2B) can look at a scanned picture of a receipt or invoice and turn it into neat computer data. Most people run them on huge, expensive server GPUs. We wanted to know: how fast and accurately can one normal 8 GB laptop/desktop GPU (RTX 4060 on Fedora Linux) do this when different engines serve the model?

1. One 8 GB desk

Single RTX 4060 (8 GB VRAM, SM 8.9), Fedora 44, no silent CPU offload, no made-up numbers.

2. 150 homemade PDFs

50 secret JSON answers written first, then turned into Digital, Clean Scan, and Smudged Scan PDFs.

3. Three engines race

Compare Transformers, vLLM, and SGLang across 1, 2, 4, and 8 simultaneous callers.

Big words, small words

    
Cheat sheet

    

      
VRAM (8 GB)
The GPU's own super-fast desk memory. Once you pass 8.0 GiB on an RTX 4060, jobs crash or spill.

      
BF16
16-bit "brain float" numbers—half the size of 32-bit floats, natively supported on RTX 4060 (SM 8.9).

      
Continuous batching
How vLLM and SGLang pack multiple callers' pages together on the GPU instead of waiting one by one.

      
KV Cache & Preemption
Scratchpad memory for active pages. At 8 callers, vLLM ran out of scratchpad 18 times and had to pause callers.

      
TTFT
Time To First Token—how long until the engine starts typing the very first character of the answer.

      
CER / WER
Character / Word Error Rate. Lower is better (0.003 CER means 99.7% of characters matched).
